Case Study

Turning a 3-Day Report Into a 15-Minute One

GRND Consulting · AI Workflow Automation · Client details anonymized for privacy

An audit firm approached me with a very specific, very familiar problem: every week, producing one internal report ate up three full working days for the staff involved.

The problem

The report itself wasn't complicated conceptually: it pulled together data the team already had, in a format partners needed to review client work. But every step was manual: exporting data from one system, reformatting it in spreadsheets, cross-referencing figures by hand, and assembling everything into a final document. None of it required judgment calls; it was the same sequence of steps, repeated identically every single week.

That's exactly the kind of process that quietly drains a team: skilled staff spending days on mechanical work instead of the actual audit judgment they were hired for.

What I built

Rather than redesigning their workflow from scratch, I automated the process they already had, recreating each manual step staff performed, in the same sequence, so the output matched what they were used to reviewing. The automation pulls the source data, applies the same formatting and cross-checks staff did by hand, and assembles the final report automatically.

That "recreate their exact steps" approach mattered: it meant no retraining, no new process to learn, and no risk of the automated output looking unfamiliar to the partners reviewing it.

The result

The report that used to take three days now takes about fifteen minutes. Staff still review the output before it goes out (automation removed the mechanical assembly work, not the professional judgment), but the days spent copying and formatting data are gone entirely.

Why it worked

The biggest automation wins usually aren't in glamorous new systems: they're in taking a process a team already trusts and removing the manual grind from it. If a report, reconciliation, or compilation task follows the same steps every time, there's a very good chance it can be automated without anyone having to change how they work.
