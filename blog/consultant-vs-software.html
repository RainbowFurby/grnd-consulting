Consultant or Just Better Software? A Simple Framework

GRND Consulting · Strategy

Not every operational headache needs a custom-built tool. Plenty of businesses hire an expensive consultant or developer for a problem an off-the-shelf app would have solved in an afternoon — and just as many buy software that never quite fits, when what they actually needed was something built around how they already work. Here's a simple way to tell which situation you're in.

Ask: does a good tool for this already exist?

If your problem is common — accounting, scheduling, basic CRM — there's very likely mature software built for exactly that, often cheaper and more reliable than building your own. If a well-known SaaS product solves 90% of the problem, buy it. Don't build what you can buy.

Ask: is the remaining 10% actually a dealbreaker?

Most "almost fits" software gets rejected over a small mismatch — a field that doesn't exist, a workflow step in the wrong order, no way to connect it to another tool you use. Before assuming you need something custom, check whether the gap can be closed with configuration, a plugin, or a small integration. This is usually far cheaper than a ground-up build.

Ask: is the process itself the actual problem?

Sometimes the software isn't the issue — the underlying process is undocumented, inconsistent, or nobody fully understands why it exists in its current form. No tool, bought or built, fixes a process nobody can clearly describe. This is where a short process audit before any building starts saves real money: it tells you what to actually solve for.

When custom actually makes sense

Building something tailored is worth it when:

  • The process is specific to how your business operates, and no off-the-shelf product fits it without heavy workarounds
  • You need two or more existing tools to talk to each other and no native integration exists
  • The manual work is high-volume and repetitive enough that even a small time saving compounds significantly
  • You're already paying for "enterprise" software just to get one feature you don't otherwise need

The honest starting point

A good consultant should be telling you when you don't need one — recommending existing software where it fits, and only proposing a custom build where it genuinely earns its cost. If someone's first answer to every problem is "let's build something," that's worth a second opinion.
