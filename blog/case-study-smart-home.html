Case Study

Cutting Invoice Turnaround From Days to Minutes

GRND Consulting · Custom Tool Building · Client details anonymized for privacy

A smart home solutions provider came to me with a problem that's common in product-based businesses: every customer inquiry set off a chain of manual work before a quote or invoice could go out.

The problem

Product information (specs, pricing, bundle options) lived across spreadsheets, WhatsApp chats, and whatever the sales team remembered from the last product briefing. Every time a customer asked "how much would it cost to set up X in my home," someone had to manually look up components, cross-check pricing, and put together a quote by hand. Simple requests took hours; more complex, multi-room setups could take days to turn into an actual invoice.

That delay wasn't just an inconvenience: in a competitive market, a slow quote is a lost sale. Customers comparing providers often went with whoever replied first.

What I built

Two connected pieces:

  • An admin portal to centralize product information (specs, pricing tiers, and bundle rules) in one place the whole team could trust, instead of scattered spreadsheets.
  • A quotation tool that lets staff select the products and options a customer wants and instantly generates an accurate, itemized quote ready to send.

The two systems talk to each other: update a price once in the admin portal, and every future quote reflects it automatically. No more quotes going out with outdated pricing, and no more hunting through old messages to remember what a similar setup cost last time.

The result

What used to take a few days from first inquiry to a ready invoice now takes minutes. Sales staff can respond to a customer in the same conversation, with an accurate number, instead of saying "let me get back to you." That speed has a direct effect on close rates. The team isn't losing deals to slower competitors anymore.

Why it worked

The fix wasn't a big enterprise system: it was purpose-built for exactly how this team already worked, sized to their actual product catalog and sales process. That's usually the difference between a tool that gets adopted and one that gets abandoned after a month: it has to fit the business, not the other way around.
